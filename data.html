(1) Methodology

Descriptive field study utilizing both quantitative and qualitative approaches.

Population: Local NGOs, traditional artisans, and community creators in Mumbai (Malad, Kandivali, etc.).
Sampling: Random stratified sampling, n = 104.
Tools: Google Forms (primary survey), unstructured interview schedules, and field observation notes.

(2) Field visit — locations & observations (Ch. 1)

Locations: online surveys shared within local networks, supported by direct interaction with creators and NGO members at workshops and offices — Malad, Kandivali, Thane, Palghar, Bhiwandi and Kumbharwada. The Google Form link let respondents from different locations participate at their convenience.

High awareness & interest. 75.9% (combining “Yes, absolutely” and “Maybe”) believe a dedicated website would help reach a wider audience — only a minority prefer strictly offline methods.

Marketing limitations. Most rely on fragmented methods — word of mouth (33.7%), social media (29.8%) — and 10.6% report no reach outside their local vicinity.

Budget & training. A large portion prefers free tools or zero budget, ~28.9% can afford only ₹1,000–5,000 monthly, and ~46.2% need introductory guidance or training. Trust preferences center on artisan stories (22.1%) and emotional connection. Photographs — artisans at workshops, NGO members at offices, survey screenshots, offline setups — support the study's authenticity.

(3) Key findings — barriers & requirements

Technical & skill: most need “a little guidance” or “full training”; order tracking and the English barrier are major difficulties. Budgetary: most prefer “nothing (free tools)” or “a little (₹1,000–5,000)” — low-maintenance solutions are essential. Trust: artisan stories and customer reviews build credibility; fraud fear persists. Support: regular maintenance and content-training sessions are highly valued post-launch.

(4) Survey design (Ch. 2)

Type: online descriptive survey via Google Forms. Target: local NGOs, artisans and creators across sectors. Sampling: random, n = 104 — link shared across networks for fair representation. Instrument: structured multiple-choice questionnaire in four parts — profile (age), website utility & marketing, training needs & digital challenges, expected features & trust elements. Pilot-tested on initial respondents for clarity before final release.

(7) Data collection plan & supporting documents

Objective: first-hand data on digital readiness, technical / financial barriers, attitudes to adoption, and feature / training suggestions. Types: primary (surveys + interviews — experience, marketing patterns, website opinions) and secondary (articles, journals, government reports, e-commerce data). Each survey took 5–10 minutes over two weeks (week 1 pilot + corrections, week 2 main collection); low-literacy respondents were guided with verbal consent.

Coverage: NGOs, artisans, creators, community workers via random online sampling. Validation: missing / inconsistent / duplicate responses removed; verified data compiled in Sheets → Excel. Supporting documents in the report annexure: questionnaire copy, interview guide + notes, field photographs and survey screenshots, visit notes, coded Excel data sheets, bar / pie charts, secondary references, consent format, project plan and surveyor guidelines.
