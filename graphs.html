(2) Data collection & analysis — from the report

Primary data came directly from local creators through the online survey and short interviews — awareness of dedicated websites, frequency of use, outreach handled digitally, difficulties in managing sites, willingness to attend training. Secondary data from articles, government publications and e-commerce reports set the background.

Each interaction lasted 10–15 minutes with verbal consent; low-literacy respondents were guided through the form. Around 104 responses were validated (missing / duplicate entries removed), compiled in Google Sheets and coded in Excel (e.g. Yes = 1, No = 0); open answers were themed into trust issues, technical problems, access gaps and positive experiences.

Quantitative analysis used percentages, frequency distribution and charts — roughly ~75% aware of digital platforms, ~55% using at least one online channel, ~45% facing network, cost or trust gaps. Qualitative interview notes were grouped by theme. Pattern: younger, smartphone-equipped creators adopt faster; older artisans prefer offline. Common barriers repeat — low digital literacy, fraud fear, poor connectivity — while most respondents welcome short, safe website-management training.

(3) Key findings

Skill. 52.0% need help (33.7% a little guidance + 18.3% full training). English barrier (29.8%) leads Q6, all four difficulties are close. Budget. 31.7% can spend over Rs. 5,000, but 24.0% want free tools — low-cost tiers required. Trust. Security badges lead (35.6%), then reviews and artisan stories (22.1% each). Support. Bug fixing leads (29.8%), then training and maintenance (24.0% each).
